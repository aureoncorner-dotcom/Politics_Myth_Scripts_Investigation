1. The central proposition
Myth can be read as processed social memory: succession, labor, kinship, sexuality, status, land, catastrophe, grief, and conflict carried through sacred narrative. “Fossilized monkey politics” is the project’s deliberately deflating phrase for that reading. “Monkey” is rhetorical language, not a taxonomic claim.
The useful extension is that myth can preserve an arrangement and become a mold for making the next arrangement. Origin, transmission, and later use are three different questions. A political use of a story does not establish that the story began as a record of that politics.
Compression, substitution, moralization, demonization, syncretic overwrite, elite editing, and trauma distortion are proposed transmission mechanisms. A resemblance suggests a comparison to investigate; it does not by itself establish a shared event, borrowing, or one secret origin.
The original confidence zones remain useful: source-grounded textual or social patterns; plausible social-memory interpretations; and deep speculative reconstructions. A tier label is not blanket verification of every sentence. Keep the passage, interpretation, and proposed historical mechanism distinguishable.

4. Priesthood-function and what survives
Priesthood-Function describes a proposed institutional sequence: capture of living activity, institutionalization, authorized narrative, obligation or debt, and enforcement. Its “narrative heist” names the rewriting of disruption into a story that legitimates the surviving order. “Embedded remainder” names what survives incorporation.
Test this at the level of concrete powers: who may interpret, who may correct, who controls access, what refusal costs, and whether the office can be bypassed or vacated. The diagnostic concerns a role or mechanism across institutions; it is not an ethnic, religious, or hereditary identity.
Survival needs specificity. A name can survive while its speaker loses authority; a ritual can persist while exit disappears. Track name, role, voice, authority, and exit separately. Distributed practice may survive a center’s fall and later concentrate again.
The Biography of an Office separates a position from its occupants. The Upkeep of Heaven asks who performs visible and invisible maintenance and who may rest or refuse. Restoration Politics asks whose chosen past is restored, whose home or agency returns, and who inherits the future work. These essays use invented thought experiments, not recovered historical cases.

5. Myth as shelter
Myth as Shelter prevents the collection from reducing every story to capture. Stories can carry mourning, dignity, pleasure, humor, companionship, privacy, and voluntary belonging. A meaningful role should leave a person able to revise it, stay silent, or leave it; care also includes the limits and rest of the people doing the caring.
The shelter essay’s house, village, and keeper are conceptual scenes. They provide questions for comparison, not evidence that a particular ancient institution worked that way. A shared good may be real even when its maintenance and authority need scrutiny.

7. The speculative branches
The Cousin-Monkey Hypothesis and Cousin hominid explore whether some stories might preserve distorted encounters with vanished populations, lineages, or powerful groups. The original essay’s monster taxonomy is another deliberately speculative branch. Preserve these as questions and creative material, with their original arguments accessible.
Mythic resemblance alone does not establish a hominin identity, deliberate heredity engineering, chromosome-fusion mechanism, or deep-time continuity. Political readings of monsters can generate tests but also fit too many stories. The project needs independent support to select one reconstruction over alternatives.
Flood memory, myth phylogeny, and body marking remain distinct research lanes. Inheritance, borrowing, convergence, symbolic invention, and later reuse remain possible explanations; this consolidation does not settle them.
