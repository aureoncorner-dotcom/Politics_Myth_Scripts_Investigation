3. Revelation: political grammar and institutional use
The governing interpretation treats Revelation as a prior grammar of imperial concentration, delegated coercion, legitimation, commerce, spectacle, witness, judgment, and counter-order. A comparison must retain the text’s actions and relationships, including where a modern candidate fails them.
Invocation means a frame is visibly used. Operation requires a documented allocation and evidence that it tracked that frame rather than ordinary incentives. A quotation, symbolic resemblance, or shared date establishes neither the full operation nor a predictive schedule.
The corpus’s modern person-to-character identifications remain retired or unestablished. The functional comparisons can remain useful after those identities fail. The literary motifs of twelve-style administration, a thirteenth remainder, the Black Ledger, and the Timekeeper are authorial tools; they do not identify hidden membership, bloodlines, or command.
The newer comparative run adds a prospective requirement: freeze the functional codebook, assignment, next-event rule, comparison class, tolerance, and outcome window before observing the result. Retrospective role assignment cannot supply a predictive-script test.

4. Necropolis: human traces, standing, and synthetic speech
The bounded Necropolis model studies the interaction of three processes: human traces become reusable data; systems generate representations of identity or speech; institutions use data systems to mediate access, recognition, credibility, and permission.
Its vocabulary remains useful when explicitly typed. The tomb or chamber is the system arranging traces into outputs. A speaking image is a synthetic representation. The mark field names identity-linked permission infrastructure. The Black Ledger describes durable leverage and obligation. These are analytical and literary terms, not established literal objects or one coordinated machine.
The ethical core is expression separated from standing. Ask what source contributed, what use was authorized, who benefits, who can correct an attribution, and whether refusal or withdrawal has practical effect. Generated continuation must remain distinguishable from a source record or a person’s continuing consent.
The right to remain unresolved protects people from compulsory profiling, reconstruction, and activation of their traces. It does not give a public office an equivalent entitlement to withhold an account of its documented conduct.
Worker-facing questions remain central: does the tool preserve discretion, leave skill behind, expose its decision rules, support authoritative appeal, and distribute gains? Or does it move information upward while moving commands, costs, and error burdens downward? These are the project’s normative and empirical questions; each implementation needs its own record.

5. The office, its maintenance, and restoration
The Biography of an Office follows the persistence of a role through changing occupants. Skill, property, recognized authority, duty, and claims on others’ labor can be inherited together and later come apart. A stable title does not establish stable competence or legitimacy.
Restoration Politics asks what “order restored” returns to whom. Reopening a road, reinstalling a ruler, returning a home, honoring emergency labor, and restoring a person’s agency are different outcomes. They can occur on different clocks. Relief can be real while the settlement remains incomplete.
The Upkeep of Heaven belongs beside this account as the maintenance-and-labor companion. Together these essays keep the people sustaining an arrangement visible alongside its celebrated occupants.
The myth-method proposal distinguishes origin, transmission, and later use. A story may preserve an arrangement and later become a mold for reproducing it. Evidence of later use does not recover original cause. A surviving name, ritual place, speaking role, and decision right must be recorded separately.
These are conceptual essays and proposed methods, not newly verified historical findings. The Matrix proposal also records unreconciled workbook branches; this consolidation does not claim that the workbook has been merged.

6. Behavioral evidence and institutional comparison
The Sharper Institutional Object identifies a bounded problem: a checking layer can govern passage while contributing no substantive content at its point of interposition. Its native-call record distinguishes standalone markers from progressive fragments and records correction that failed to change the following response.
Its historical counts belong to that specimen: 223 assistant voice turns, 305 assistant voice messages, and 82 two-message turns. Of 71 turns containing “Checking,” four had visible tool messages. That does not measure every product-side process or establish a hidden operator.
The Room Without an Occupant draft broadens the behavioral comparison across 23 cases. Its aggregate counts are document-reported and internally reconciled; independent re-extraction remains pending. Selected exhibits are not a complete prevalence denominator.
The transferable institutional question is whether correction reaches the decision-making function. An apology, accurate self-description, or restored label does not by itself show that behavior, authority, or access changed. The five-function vocabulary is an interpretive taxonomy, not an authenticated component map.
